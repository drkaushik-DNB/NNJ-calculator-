Rules used by the calculator

Neurotoxicity risk factors (AAP 2022 Table 2)

  • Gestational age under 38 weeks (risk rises with prematurity; handled by the separate curves)
  • Albumin below 3.0 g/dL
  • Isoimmune hemolytic disease (positive DAT), G6PD deficiency, or another hemolytic condition
  • Septicemia
  • Significant clinical instability in the previous 24 hours

Treatment levels

  • Use TSB, not TcB, for treatment decisions. Do not subtract direct bilirubin.
  • Start intensive phototherapy at or above the hour-specific threshold. An infant under 24 h with a TSB at or above the threshold likely has a hemolytic process.
  • Escalation of care begins at the exchange threshold minus 2 mg/dL: NICU, intensive phototherapy, IV hydration, TSB at least every 2 h, STAT labs including albumin, type and crossmatch.
  • Urgent exchange transfusion at or above the exchange threshold, or with signs of intermediate or advanced acute bilirubin encephalopathy at any level.
  • Bilirubin to albumin ratio (TSB mg/dL divided by albumin g/dL), exchange may be considered at or above: 8.0 for 38 weeks and above without risk factors; 7.2 for 38 weeks and above with a risk factor, or 35 to 37 weeks without; 6.8 for 35 to 37 weeks with a risk factor.
  • IVIG 0.5 to 1 g/kg over 2 h in isoimmune hemolysis (DAT positive) at or above the escalation level; can be repeated in 12 h.

During and after phototherapy (AAP 2022)

  • Hospital: TSB within 12 h of starting, then by age, risk factors, level and trend. A TcB under the lights underestimates.
  • Stopping is an option when TSB is at least 2 mg/dL below the threshold at the hour phototherapy started. Treat longer if rebound risk: gestational age under 38 weeks, started before 48 h, or hemolytic disease.
  • Exceeded the threshold during the birth stay and (phototherapy before 48 h, DAT positive, or hemolysis): TSB 6 to 12 h after stopping and again the next day.
  • Other infants treated in the birth stay: bilirubin the next day. Readmitted or home phototherapy: bilirubin or clinical review in 1 to 2 days. Allow at least 12 h, preferably 24 h, after stopping. TcB is acceptable 24 h or more after stopping.
  • Home LED phototherapy needs all of: 38 weeks or more, 48 h or more old, clinically well with adequate feeding, no neurotoxicity risk factor, no previous phototherapy, TSB no more than 1 mg/dL above the threshold, a device available without delay, and TSB daily.

Before treatment: follow-up by distance below the threshold (AAP 2022 Figure 7)

Figure 7 applies from 12 h of age, in infants who have not received phototherapy. An infant discharged before 12 h needs a bilirubin between 24 and 48 h.

Screening and hemolysis flags

  • TSB or TcB between 24 and 48 h of age, or before discharge if earlier. Jaundice in the first 24 h needs a TcB or TSB as soon as possible.
  • Check a TSB if the TcB is 15 mg/dL or more, or within 3 mg/dL of the phototherapy threshold.
  • A rise of 0.3 mg/dL/h or more in the first 24 h, or 0.2 mg/dL/h or more after, suggests hemolysis: check DAT.
  • Direct bilirubin above 50% of TSB: consult a specialist.

NICE (preterm, and as a comparison for term)

  • 38 weeks or more and over 24 h old, below the phototherapy threshold but within 50 µmol/L: repeat bilirubin within 18 h if a sibling needed phototherapy or the mother plans exclusive breastfeeding, otherwise within 24 h. More than 50 µmol/L below: do not routinely repeat.
  • During phototherapy: repeat serum bilirubin 4 to 6 h after starting; if stable or falling, every 6 to 12 h.
  • Stop phototherapy when bilirubin is at least 50 µmol/L below the threshold. Check for rebound 12 to 18 h after stopping.
  • Intensify (multiple phototherapy units) if bilirubin rises faster than 8.5 µmol/L per hour, is within 50 µmol/L of the exchange threshold, or does not fall within 6 h of starting.

Where the numbers come from, and their limits

  • AAP values come from Supplemental Tables 1 to 4 in the guideline PDF you supplied (Kemper et al., Pediatrics 2022;150(3):e2022058859), read hour by hour from 1 to 336 h. Hour 0 is not tabulated, so the 1 h value is used. Between whole hours the page interpolates in a straight line.
  • The original exchange curves are dotted for the first 24 h because the guideline marks them uncertain.
  • The figures shown on the charts tab and here are cropped from the same PDF.
  • For 35 to 37 weeks the NICE comparison card uses the NICE phototherapy formula; its exchange level there is an estimate (phototherapy plus 100 µmol/L from 72 h) because NICE publishes it only as graphs.
  • The 34-weeks-or-lower cut-offs (23 to 34 weeks, at 12, 24, 48 and 72 h or more) follow the NICE 2023 guideline table, not the AAP 2022 guideline, which covers 35 weeks and above only. Values between listed ages are interpolated; before 12 h the 12 h value is used.
  • This is a decision-support aid for clinicians. Confirm treatment decisions against the guideline and your unit protocol.
